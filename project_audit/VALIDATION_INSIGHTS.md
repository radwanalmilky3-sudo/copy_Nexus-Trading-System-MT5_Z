## Multiple-testing correction is the default assumption, not an afterthought

Any research program that searches a space of candidate factors, signal variants, parameter grids, or ML configurations is running many hypothesis tests, and the moment you report the best one you have implicitly selected on noise. The reference system treats this as the central problem of strategy research: raw p-values are never the decision object. Instead, every family of tests (all features screened in a fold, all candidates in an event study, all instruments in a panel) is first declared as a family, and the raw p-values are converted into False Discovery Rate adjusted values — Benjamini–Hochberg when tests are independent or positively dependent, Benjamini–Yekutieli when arbitrary dependence must be tolerated. The practical consequence is that a candidate is only "significant" if its adjusted value clears a pre-set FDR level (commonly 5–10%), and a secondary, more conservative correction is usually reported alongside the primary one as a sensitivity check. The deeper principle is that the correction must be computed over the *full declared family*, including the tests that failed — dropping losers from the denominator is how FDR control silently breaks. A second, complementary adjustment is the deflated-Sharpe idea: rather than asking "is this Sharpe positive?", ask "given that I tried N strategies whose Sharpe statistics have an observed mean and dispersion, is this one better than the best Sharpe I would expect to see by chance from that trial universe?" The comparison is done at daily frequency with non-normality (skewness and kurtosis) accounted for, and it is benchmarked against the *expected maximum* Sharpe of the disclosed trial count. Both mechanisms share one philosophy: the number of attempts is itself data, and it must be disclosed, frozen, and fed into the statistic.

## Hansen SPA is a family-level guardrail, not a winner picker

The Hansen (2005) Superior Predictive Ability test answers a specific question: "is the best-performing candidate in this frozen family genuinely better than the benchmark, after accounting for the fact that I picked the maximum out of many?" The reference implementation is deliberately framed as a diagnostic that does *not* select a candidate and does *not* approve trading — it returns a family-level rejection or non-rejection, and identifying a winner is a separate, later decision. Several design choices encode important principles. First, the inputs must be pre-frozen cost-adjusted daily net-return series on an *identical* common date index; a candidate that is missing dates, has infinities, or is aligned to a different calendar is rejected outright rather than silently truncated, because silent alignment differences are one of the most common ways backtests become incomparable. Second, the statistic is studentized: each candidate-minus-benchmark differential is scaled by its own long-run variance, so candidates with different volatility and autocorrelation profiles are compared on a common scale rather than by raw mean return. Third, the test is inherently about the *maximum* of a set of standardized mean differentials, so the bootstrap must recenter each candidate under the null using one of three rules — a liberal rule that truncates negatives at zero, a least-favourable rule that recenters at the raw mean, and a recommended sample-dependent rule that zeros out means below a slowly vanishing threshold of order sqrt(LRV/T · log log T). Reporting all three as a lower/consistent/upper triple turns the recentering choice, usually a hidden researcher degree of freedom, into a disclosed sensitivity band, and a hard invariant asserts that the three p-values must be ordered. The lesson for any model-selection pipeline: state the family, state the benchmark, state the null, and report the whole band of reasonable answers rather than the single most favorable one.

## Bootstrap methodology decides whether the p-values mean anything

Daily financial return series are not iid — they exhibit volatility clustering, autocorrelation in squares, and cross-sectional co-movement — so iid resampling produces confidently wrong p-values. The reference system therefore uses *block* resampling throughout: circular fixed-length blocks, and a stationary bootstrap where blocks restart with a geometrically distributed length whose mean is a declared parameter (in practice a quarter of a trading year, ~63 days). The block length is a statistical assumption, not a tuning knob: too short and you destroy the dependence you were trying to preserve; too long and the effective number of independent resamples collapses. The system requires at least two full expected blocks of data before it will run at all, which is an honest refusal to compute inference on a sample too short to support the assumed dependence structure. Two further principles matter enormously. The resampling must be *joint*: one index path is drawn per bootstrap replicate and applied to every candidate and instrument column simultaneously, preserving contemporaneous dependence across the panel — resampling each column independently would fabricate diversification and understate the variance of the maximum, which is exactly the quantity SPA tests. And p-values must use the plus-one correction, counting exceedances with `>=` ties and dividing by (reps + 1), which guarantees a strictly positive, finite-sample-valid p-value rather than the biased zero you get from a naive ratio. Because a Monte Carlo p-value is itself an estimate, its standard error (roughly sqrt(p(1−p)/(reps+1))) is reported next to it, and a companion helper computes the *minimum* number of resamples needed for the smallest achievable plus-one p-value to even reach the first FDR threshold given the family size — otherwise a 10,000-replicate bootstrap can never resolve a 5% threshold over a family of hundreds of hypotheses, and "not significant" becomes an artifact of insufficient computation rather than of evidence. Reproducibility is a statistical requirement too: fixed seeds, batch-size-invariant random streams (so changing memory batching does not change the answer), and explicit reporting of seed, reps, and block length make a result re-derivable by anyone.

## Significance thresholds must be pre-declared, resolvable, and layered

A threshold chosen after seeing the results is not a threshold, it is a narrative. The reference project's discipline is that the FDR level, the primary and sensitivity correction methods, the block length, the resample count, and the candidate family are all declared before results are examined, and the system computes up front whether the chosen resample budget can actually resolve that threshold at that family size — failing loudly rather than reporting a floor-limited p-value as evidence. Layering is the other half: instead of a single binary "significant" flag, the gates are stacked — positive mean excess return, BH significance, BY sensitivity significance, a negative-control gate (a placebo or deliberately-destroyed series must *fail* to pass, verifying the pipeline can distinguish signal from noise), and an undefined-when-impossible status for the deflated Sharpe when the trial universe is too small or degenerate to define it. An undefined statistic is reported as undefined with a reason, never coerced to a convenient number. Thresholds are also kept in their proper units: Sharpe ratios and their deflated/probabilistic variants are compared at daily frequency with no annualization applied mid-comparison, because annualizing some candidates and not others, or annualizing a statistic whose null distribution was derived at daily frequency, silently inflates apparent significance. Finally, the ordering of evidence matters — a family-level SPA rejection, an FDR-selected row, and a passing deflated-Sharpe probability are all still labeled as *research diagnostics*, never as approvals, because statistical significance is a statement about a sample and not a commitment about the future.

## Preventing overfitting: freeze first, then test, then hold out

The most reliable anti-overfitting controls are procedural rather than mathematical. In the reference system, candidates, factors, cut-offs, cost assumptions, and thresholds are frozen at a declared research-freeze point; anything chosen after outcomes are visible is tainted and cannot be part of the tested family. The system explicitly distinguishes *reused history* from *untouched holdout*: a decade of data that has been repeatedly mined for signals is not a holdout no matter how you slice it, so genuine forward validation must be strictly later than the alpha freeze. Supporting diagnostics reinforce this. Purged and embargoed cross-validation prevents information leakage between train and test windows when labels overlap. Selection-frequency gates require that a candidate be selected consistently across folds rather than winning on one lucky split. Combined-chrozen (CSCV) style splits produce a Probability of Backtest Overfitting estimate — the probability that the in-sample best would have underperformed out-of-sample — which quantifies how likely the whole selection procedure, not just one model, was to have overfit. Negative controls and placebo series test that the pipeline rejects garbage; registries record how many hypotheses were actually tried, so the deflated-Sharpe trial count is honest. External data must carry availability timestamps, versions, and hashes so nothing that post-dates a decision point can leak backward into it. And the failure mode of "narrow the universe by dropping the instruments that failed" is prohibited, because shrinking the tested family after the fact is a multiple-testing violation in disguise. The unifying idea: overfitting is not prevented by a cleverer statistic but by making it impossible to move the goalposts after the fact.

## How validation gates promotion to live

The reference project's promotion architecture is explicitly fail-closed and staged: research gates (FDR, deflated Sharpe, negative controls) produce a *research verdict*; only a research verdict that passes can advance to a frozen-model stage where the selected specification is refit once; only that frozen model can advance to forward paper validation; and trading approval is a separate, user-authorized state that defaults to false and is never set by a research routine. Every artifact carries the approval flags as literal false values, and a manifest re-verification step recomputes declared artifact hashes and re-asserts that approval has not been flipped — so a stale or tampered result cannot silently inherit approval status. CLI surfaces print the current approval state alongside results, and messages state plainly that a passed gate "still requires forward paper validation" while a failed gate means the candidate is not promotable. This layering exists because each layer answers a different question: statistical significance asks whether the sample evidence is distinguishable from noise; the deflated/SPA corrections ask whether it survives the selection process that produced it; PBO asks whether the selection procedure itself overfits; forward paper asks whether it survives contact with genuinely unseen data; and only a human decision, with real cost assumptions and real execution frictions validated, converts any of that into live capital. The transferable principle is that the gate must be a *choke point* — a single code path through which promotion must pass, with the approval flag as machine-verifiable state that defaults to off, cannot be set by the component being promoted, and is re-checked at load time rather than trusted from a stored file.

## Key Principles for Nexus

- **Declare the family before you test.** Record every strategy/feature/hyperparameter trial in a research registry with counts, thresholds, and cost assumptions frozen up front; the deflated-Sharpe trial count and the FDR denominator must reflect *all* attempts, including failures.
- **Adjust, never read raw p-values.** Convert bootstrap p-values to BH FDR q-values as the primary gate and BY as a dependence-robust sensitivity check; a candidate passes only if it clears the pre-set FDR level under the primary method and does not contradict the sensitivity method.
- **Size the computation to the threshold.** Compute the minimum resamples needed for the smallest plus-one p-value to reach the FDR level at the declared family size; refuse to report "not significant" when the resolution floor prevents it, and report the Monte Carlo standard error next to every bootstrap p-value.
- **Resample jointly in blocks.** Use block/stationary bootstrap with a declared expected block length on a strictly common date index; one shared index path per replicate across all candidates and instruments, plus-one p-values with `>=` ties, fixed seeds, and batch-size-invariant streams.
- **Add a family-level selection guardrail.** Run a Hansen-SPA-style test of "best frozen candidate vs benchmark" that reports lower/consistent/upper recentering bounds and is labeled a family diagnostic — it must never name a winner or grant approval.
- **Layer negative controls and honesty checks.** Require a placebo/negative-control series to fail, report undefined statistics as undefined with a reason, and never narrow the tested universe or move cut-offs/thresholds after outcomes are visible.
- **Freeze, then hold out honestly.** Treat all mined history as reused; forward validation must be strictly later than the alpha freeze, use purged/embargoed splits for any refit, and be accompanied by a PBO-style estimate of how likely the selection procedure was to overfit.
- **Make the gate a choke point with fail-closed state.** Introduce a single promotion path into live/ML-agent deployment where an approval flag defaults to false, cannot be set by the component being promoted, is re-verified (including artifact hashes) at load time, and requires an explicit human authorization to flip.
